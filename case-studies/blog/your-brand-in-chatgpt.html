What does your brand look like inside ChatGPT when a shopper asks for it?

The campaigns your team already runs across paid, owned, earned, and retail media are landing shoppers inside AI conversations. The question isn't whether your brand will be discussed there. It's whether you'll show up controlled, accurate, and ready to be chosen, or whether the response will be generic.

Why are shoppers starting purchase journeys inside ChatGPT?

For the last two decades, a shopper's journey began in a search bar or on a category page. That habit is changing fast. In its April 2026 report on agentic commerce, Make Your Brand Unmissable, Accenture finds that AI platforms have already overtaken physical stores as the number-one channel shoppers turn to for advice, and that within the next 18 to 24 months a majority of consumers will complete purchases inside AI conversations that did not begin with an intent to buy.

That is a generational change in where demand gets formed, and it changes what a brand actually has to be ready for. People are bringing real questions into ChatGPT every day: what should I try for sensitive skin, what would go with a navy linen suit for a beach wedding, will this sofa fit a 9 by 12 living room. When a brand surfaces in that conversation, the experience the shopper has next is what decides whether the brand gets chosen or skipped over.

"85% of consumers are likely to abandon an online purchase due to frustration or indecision, and more than three in four feel overwhelmed by too many options."
Accenture, Make Your Brand Unmissable

How big is the shift to AI-mediated shopping in the next 18 to 24 months?

Accenture's simulation, run with Aaru across 50,000 synthetic consumers in 24 countries, projects that up to 45% of shoppers will shift at least half of their commerce activity into agent-mediated experiences over the next 18 to 24 months. The same simulation surfaced a finding that brands often miss: up to 86% of agent-mediated transactions are at risk of being abandoned or switched to a competitor when something goes wrong in the purchase process. Agents do not tolerate ambiguity, missing facts, or broken handoffs the way human shoppers occasionally do.

Which industries are seeing the fastest AI shopping adoption?

The fastest acceleration is in categories where a single decision involves real complexity: beauty and personal care, travel, and health and wellness. These are the categories where the friction of digital commerce has always been highest, and they are the categories where shoppers are most likely to ask an AI assistant for guidance before they ever land on a product page.

The same pattern is forming everywhere a purchase requires a moment of thought, including luxury fashion, home and living, electronics, and alcohol and spirits. Wherever a shopper would once have asked a salesperson, a friend, or a search engine, a growing share of them are starting to ask an AI agent instead.

What happens when a shopper brings your brand into an AI conversation?

When a shopper types @yourbrand inside ChatGPT, or lands in a conversation through a link from a campaign your team ran, something has to render on the screen. Without a brand-controlled surface, what renders is a generic response composed from whatever the model has picked up about you in the wild. There is no merchandising, no imagery you approved, no way to enforce category-specific rules like age gates or allergy disclosures, and no guarantee about where the shopper gets sent next.

With a brand-controlled surface inside ChatGPT, that same moment renders very differently. The experience uses your visual identity, your approved imagery, and your verified product facts; it applies the guardrails your category requires; and it hands off cleanly to the destination you have already chosen for the final step of the journey, whether that is your direct-to-consumer site, a verified retailer partner, a marketplace listing, or a physical store appointment.

Why does the moment your brand enters a conversation matter more than the click?

In the old digital model, the click was the moment of truth: the shopper clicked a search result, an ad, or a social post, and landed on a product page the brand fully controlled. Agentic commerce moves the moment of truth upstream. The conversation itself is the storefront, and by the time the shopper would have clicked anything, the AI agent has already evaluated, compared, and decided whether to surface the brand at all. The brand's influence has to be present inside the conversation, not waiting at the end of it.

What is the difference between a generic AI response and a brand-controlled experience?

A generic AI response is whatever the model decides to produce based on incomplete public data. It might be roughly right, but it is not yours: no merchandising, no priority on the products you actually want to lead with, no acknowledgement of the disclosures and disclaimers your category requires. A brand-controlled experience uses verified product data, brand-approved imagery, and category-specific guardrails, and serves all of it through structured, machine-readable signals the agent can trust. The difference shows up as confidence on the agent's side and clarity on the shopper's side.

What does an AI agent actually need to recommend a brand confidently?

Accenture frames this as the "choice of agents" problem: most brands now have to structure their offerings to be seen and chosen by AI agents. The agents are not human, but the criteria are surprisingly familiar. They want clear facts, claims they can verify, and a low chance of getting the recommendation wrong.

"Don't ask agents to believe your story. Give them reasons to choose you, repeatedly… Every product listing should spell out what the product 'works for' or 'doesn't work for.'"
Accenture, Make Your Brand Unmissable

What product information do AI shopping agents look for?

Four things, consistently:

  1. Structured facts about the product itself, including composition, dimensions, ingredients, certifications, compatibility, and allergens.
  2. Verifiable claims, meaning anything the brand says about performance, sourcing, or outcomes that can actually be backed up.
  3. Fit guidance, with explicit "works for" and "does not work for" signals that let the agent confidently rule a product in or out of a recommendation without resorting to guesswork.
  4. Post-purchase clarity, covering return policies, warranty terms, service availability, and the actual destinations where a shopper can complete the purchase.

What is Contextual Rank, and how do brands earn it?

Contextual Rank is the agentic-era equivalent of search rank. It describes a brand's standing inside AI conversations: how confidently an agent will surface the brand when a relevant question comes up, and how completely the agent can answer the shopper's follow-ups using the brand's own data. It is not bought, and it is not granted by the model. It is earned the same way SEO authority was earned, through exposing well-structured, accurate, machine-readable product data and through performing reliably across the whole post-purchase tail.

The brands building Contextual Rank today are doing two things at once. They are making themselves legible to agents through structured product data and clearly written policies, and they are shaping a brand-controlled experience that takes over when their name comes up in a conversation. Brands that do neither get summarized by the model: imperfectly, generically, and without recourse.

How is being chosen by an AI agent different from ranking on Google?

Google ranks pages against a query and shows ten links on a results page. An AI agent answers a question and decides what to surface, in what order, and whether to surface a brand at all. There is no page two, and there is no scroll. There is a single answer, and the brands present in that answer get the consideration. Everything else is, for that shopper in that moment, invisible.

What does an agent-ready brand experience look like in skincare?

Skincare is one of the categories Accenture flags as accelerating fastest into agent-mediated shopping. It is also one of the categories where the cost of getting a recommendation wrong is highest: the wrong ingredient for a sensitivity, the wrong actives stacked together, the wrong product for a condition. A generic AI response that fails to surface the right disclaimers, allergens, or patch-test guidance is a real safety issue, not just a marketing problem.

A brand-controlled experience handles this directly. When a shopper says they have sensitive skin or a specific condition, the agent does not have to guess at fragrance status or active concentrations, because the brand has already exposed those answers in a form the agent can trust.

Notice that the agent did not have to interpret marketing copy or stitch together information from multiple sources. It read the brand's structured facts, applied the shopper's stated condition, and produced a confident answer that names the actives, calls out what the formulation avoids, flags the relevant patch-test guidance, and tells the shopper why this specific product is the right fit for rosacea-prone skin. The brand's expertise is present inside the conversation, not buried two clicks away on a product page the shopper may never reach.

What does it look like in luxury fashion?

Luxury fashion is a different kind of problem. The shopper is not optimizing the way they might in a grocery basket; they are making a considered, often emotional purchase, frequently with questions about provenance, craftsmanship, pairing across the maison, and how to actually buy. The category has always been built on tactile, in-person service, and the real question for luxury brands is how to bring that level of considered guidance into an AI conversation without flattening it into a spec sheet.

What an agent needs here is richer than a parts list. It needs material composition with sourcing context, care guidance the brand actually stands behind, pairing suggestions across the brand's own line, and a clean path to a private appointment at a flagship when the shopper is ready to see the piece in person.

Look at what the agent did not do here. It did not quote a third-party blog about silk weight, and it did not send the shopper to a marketplace listing with a stock photo. It surfaced the maison's own knowledge in the maison's own voice, suggested pieces across the brand's line that the team would actually want paired with the dress, and finished by booking a private appointment, which is the kind of hand-off luxury commerce has always relied on, now made native to the conversation.

What about home and living brands?

Home and living has the highest "will it fit" tax in commerce. A shopper can fall in love with a sofa, a dining table, or a sectional, and still abandon the purchase because they are not sure about dimensions, clearance, material wear, or whether the colorway in the photo is the colorway they will actually receive at home. Returns in this category are punishing for brands and exhausting for customers, and agents already know that.

A brand-controlled response in this category is mostly a story about specificity. Exact dimensions including assembled depth, material composition broken down across frame, weave, fill, and finish, colorways with the names the brand actually uses, lead times that match what production can support, and white-glove delivery availability by zip. The shopper's real question is "will this work in my home," and the answer the agent needs to surface has to be structured enough to be confident.

The shopper got a confident yes-or-no on fit, the materials they would otherwise have had to dig through a product page to find, and a pairing the brand actually wanted to surface. None of that came from the model guessing; it came from the brand exposing it in a form the agent could use.

What visibility should brands ask for in return?

Here is a question almost no brand is asking yet, and Accenture is one of the first analyst firms to put it plainly. If a brand is going to expose its product catalog, policies, and performance data to an AI agent, it should get something back in return: visibility into how that data is being used, why the brand is winning or losing recommendations, and where the friction is costing it sales.

"Exposing first-party data without getting something in return puts brands at a disadvantage. Leading brands will insist on having visibility into how their products or services are being evaluated, why they won or lost a recommendation, how their prices compare and where friction is costing them sales."
Accenture, Make Your Brand Unmissable

Why do brands need signal back from AI conversations?

Without it, a brand has no way to improve and no leverage in the relationship. The brands that win the next decade of commerce will not just be the ones that make themselves agent-readable. They will be the ones that close the loop, using the signal coming back from AI conversations to refine product data, sharpen category guidance, fix the most common shopper objections at the source, and keep their Contextual Rank compounding over time.

How should brands prepare for the AI conversations their campaigns are already starting?

The good news is that none of this requires waiting for a future protocol or a new channel to mature. The shoppers are already in the conversations, the campaigns your team is running are already landing them there, and the work is the preparation.

What should you do this quarter to be ready?

Three things, in order:

  1. Audit your product data through an agent's eyes. If your ingredients, dimensions, materials, allergens, and certifications are buried inside marketing copy and PDFs rather than structured and machine-readable, an agent cannot quote what it cannot parse.
  2. Decide what your brand-controlled response actually looks like when a shopper brings your name into an AI conversation. That includes what renders on the screen, which guardrails apply, and what the path looks like from the conversation to your approved destination, whether that destination is your DTC site, a verified retailer, a marketplace listing, or a store appointment.
  3. Instrument the loop. Treat the AI channel the way your team already treats search and social, with clear measurement, a defined set of KPIs, and a feedback path from conversation signal back into product data and category strategy.

What is at stake if you wait?

Accenture frames the stakes more bluntly than most analyst firms do, and the framing is worth quoting in full.

"Brands that get this right, with upstream and downstream execution that is nearly flawless and content and infrastructure that meets the needs of people and agents, have the potential for once-in-a-generation growth and profit. The rest risk becoming invisible to consumers and the agents working on their behalf."
Accenture, Make Your Brand Unmissable

Invisible, in this context, is not about being hidden on the open web. It is about the moment when a shopper does bring your brand into a conversation and the response that renders has nothing of your brand in it. That moment is already happening, in every category, in every market where ChatGPT is being used as a shopping advisor. The brands that prepare for it now will be the ones that get chosen as the channel matures.
