The constraint

Delivery platforms run on relevance.

Show someone a restaurant that's closed, or a product that's not available in their area, and the click is wasted. And the advertiser notices. When the platform is serving 50+ CPG brand partners and 250+ restaurant partners simultaneously, relevance isn't a polish item; it is the substrate the entire marketplace stands on.

Generic brand-level creative was eroding advertiser confidence on both sides, because the creative that ran didn't reflect the state of the world the shopper was actually clicking into. A unified surface needed unified, live signals behind every impression, and the existing production model couldn't carry that weight.

The signal gap

Brand-level creative can't carry store-level truth.

A delivery marketplace lives at the intersection of two moving inventories: what the restaurant is actually serving right now, and what the CPG partner actually has on shelf in this shopper's market. Either signal going stale turns an otherwise well-targeted impression into a dead end, and the platform absorbs the cost on the advertiser's behalf.

Generic creative built once and shipped everywhere is structurally unable to reflect either signal. The relevance problem wasn't a creative-quality problem; it was a wiring problem between the ad surface and the live state of the marketplace.

The rebuild

Live inventory, live geo, live product availability, on one engine.

DaVinci Commerce's real-time decisioning engine powered creative that updated automatically with live inventory, geolocation, and product availability, across programmatic and social in the same run. Each impression resolved against the current state of the partner it was promoting, so closed restaurants and out-of-stock SKUs simply stopped surfacing in the markets where they wouldn't convert.

Category-specific templates let the platform scale across its entire partner portfolio without rebuilding for each campaign. A new CPG partner or a new restaurant cohort plugged into the same decisioning fabric and inherited the same live-signal logic on day one, rather than spinning up a parallel production track.
