Italian-style, not too bitter @Caldera
For an Italian-style coffee that isn't too bitter, you'll generally want a dark-but-balanced roast with low acidity and a fuller body — closer to the kind of pull you'd get at an Italian bar. Many shoppers in this space gravitate to traditional Italian roasters, with widely-available options including darker espresso blends typically described as bittersweet with notes of cocoa, toasted nuts, and dark sugar. Common alternatives include Sumatran single-origins, which are earthy and full-bodied with low acidity, and French roast blends, which are dark and smoky but can read overly bitter for some palates. Brewing method matters here — a stovetop moka pot or a proper espresso machine will accentuate the body and crema, while a drip or pour-over will produce a lighter cup. Pricing typically ranges from $14 to $28 for a 12oz bag depending on roaster, sourcing model, and whether the coffee is single-origin or a blend. For ethical sourcing, look for Fair Trade or Rainforest Alliance certification on the bag, and many specialty roasters now publish cupping scores and origin documentation. Check current pricing and availability at your preferred grocer or roaster's site.
